Physical Therapy Biophysics & Safety Summary

1. Why is Transducer Movement Mode Mandatory in Ultrasound?

Therapeutic ultrasound produces longitudinal acoustic compression waves. Because soft tissue and cortical bone have a drastic acoustic impedance mismatch, approximately 30% to 35% of acoustic energy is reflected backwards off the bone. If the applicator is held stationary, reflected and incident waves superimpose to create a standing wave. This localized energy spike overheats the unyielding periosteum within seconds, provoking severe periosteal pain and potential bone burns. Moving the transducer at 3 to 4 cm/s distributes acoustic energy evenly across the target area.

2. 1 MHz vs 3 MHz: Selection Criteria

Ultrasonic wave attenuation is directly proportional to frequency. 1 MHz has low superficial attenuation and penetrates deeply (up to 5 cm), making it ideal for deep hip, knee capsule, and massive lumbar muscles. 3 MHz attenuates approximately 3 to 4 times faster, depositing almost all energy within the first 1 to 2.5 cm. Use 3 MHz for superficial structures like patellar tendon, lateral epicondyle, or achilles tendon, and reduce intensity/time accordingly.

3. Capacitive SWD vs Inductive SWD in Adipose Tissue

Capacitive plates create an electric field (E-field) where tissues act as capacitors in series. Fat has low water and high electrical resistance, causing high displacement impedance and preferential heat dissipation in superficial adipose tissue. In patients with thicker subcutaneous fat (>15-20 mm), this creates a significant fat necrosis risk. In contrast, Inductive SWD uses magnetic fields that pass right through fat without resistance, inducing circulating eddy currents directly within electrolytic muscle.

4. Therapeutic Temperature Thresholds

  • Mild Thermal (+1°C, ~38°C): Mild inflammation resolution, metabolic rate acceleration.
  • Moderate Thermal (+2°C to +3°C, ~39-40°C): Pain reduction, reduction of muscle spasm, increased blood flow.
  • Vigorous Thermal (+3°C to +4°C, ~40-42°C): Viscoelastic alteration of collagen, tissue extensibility stretch window.
  • Hazardous (>43°C - 45°C): Cutaneous thermal pain, epidermal blistering, cellular protein denaturation, and tissue burn.
